ReMatch: Return-to-Resale

A second-place project at the McGill Bensadoun Retail Gen-AI Hackathon (October 2026). The prototype explores how AI-assisted orchestration and explicit business rules could help screen returned footwear for resale.

The business question

When a returned product is still potentially sellable, can a structured workflow verify inspection and resale approval, screen a synthetic buyer waitlist against eligibility constraints, and prepare a simulated offer? The prototype addresses operational decision consistency, not proven resale demand or profit maximization.

Decision architecture

Conceptual diagram of the demonstrated decision logic; not a production system architecture. Inspection and pricing are provided as inputs, not inferred by an image model or optimized by the agent.

What the AI does — and does not do

The Strands/Bedrock agent coordinates tool calls. Python business rules govern inspection status, authorization, buyer eligibility, and offer reuse. The agent does not establish real buyer intent, independently grade a physical item, or guarantee an actual sale. A separate historical-case Streamlit demo runs without AWS, using locally held case fixtures and synthetic buyer inputs.

Testing and evidence

In a local macOS run reported November 2026, 82 automated tests passed: 28 agent/offline, 32 notifications, and 22 historical-demo tests. Covered scenarios include blocked inspections, buyer opt-out, grade and willingness-to-pay limits, duplicate offer reuse, and backend eligibility revalidation. This does not establish AWS end-to-end reliability, security certification, or production readiness.

Business limitations and next validation

All test buyers and willingness-to-pay ceilings are fictional. Net recovery is an illustrative estimate from proposed price minus modeled costs, not realized profit. To establish commercial value, a future pilot would need authorized customer research, measured conversion, actual reverse-logistics costs, and operational review.
