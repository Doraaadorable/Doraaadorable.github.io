ReMatch: Return-to-Resale

2nd of 10 finalist teams at the McGill Bensadoun Retail Gen-AI Hackathon (October 2026): an agent that decides whether a returned pair is resold, liquidated or donated, and matches resale stock to eligible buyers under explicit business rules.

The business question

When a returned pair can still be resold, can a structured workflow check inspection and resale approval, screen a buyer waitlist against eligibility rules, and prepare an offer, the same way every time? The prototype targets consistent operational decisions; resale demand and prices enter as inputs.

My role

I was the modeller in a six-person team, wrote the code and built the Streamlit dashboard.

Decision architecture

Decision logic of the prototype. Inspection results and proposed prices are inputs to the workflow.

How decisions are made

The Strands agent on AWS Bedrock chooses which tool to call next. Python rules decide inspection status, authorization, buyer eligibility, price and offer reuse, so every decision traces to a rule. At a fixed price every eligible buyer yields the same recovery, so buyers are asked in order of headroom, ceiling minus price: the least price-sensitive first.

Resell, liquidate or donate

Resale has a cost even when it fails: cleaning and handling are paid up front, and a pair that does not sell within the listing window still goes to a liquidator or donation after a holding cost. With price p, shipping s, preparation c, holding cost h and A the better net value of liquidation or donation, resale beats the alternatives only when the sell-through rate exceeds

q* = (c + h) / (p − s − A + h)

In the demo case (price 55, liquidation netting 16) the break-even is 34%: resale wins at 60% sell-through, liquidation at 30%. The dashboard lets every input be changed and also sets a price from buyer ceilings: the highest price that at least two buyers will accept, so a backup buyer remains.

Testing

79 automated tests pass in CI, together with a render check of the dashboard. They cover blocked inspections, buyer opt-out, grade and willingness-to-pay limits, buyer order, duplicate offers, notification consent and dry-run sending, and the disposition model on both sides of the break-even.

From prototype to pilot

The public version runs on synthetic buyers, ceilings and costs. The quantity a pilot has to measure is sell-through by grade and price: with it and actual reverse-logistics costs, the break-even rate turns into a routing rule per return.
