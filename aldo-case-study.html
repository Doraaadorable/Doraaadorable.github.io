ReMatch: Return-to-Resale

Second place at the McGill Bensadoun Retail Gen-AI Hackathon (October 2026): an AI-assisted workflow that screens returned footwear for resale and matches it to eligible buyers under explicit business rules.

The business question

When a returned pair can still be resold, can a structured workflow check inspection and resale approval, screen a buyer waitlist against eligibility rules, and prepare an offer, the same way every time? The prototype targets consistent operational decisions; resale demand and prices enter as inputs.

My role

I was the modeller in a six-person team. I also wrote code for the prototype and built its Streamlit dashboard.

Decision architecture

Decision logic of the prototype. Inspection results and proposed prices are inputs to the workflow.

How decisions are made

The Strands agent on AWS Bedrock coordinates the tool calls. Python business rules decide inspection status, authorization, buyer eligibility and offer reuse, so every decision can be traced to a rule. A separate Streamlit demo replays historical cases without AWS.

Testing

82 automated tests pass in a local run (November 2026): 28 agent and offline, 32 notification and 22 historical-demo tests. They cover blocked inspections, buyer opt-out, grade and willingness-to-pay limits, duplicate offers and backend eligibility revalidation.

From prototype to pilot

The prototype runs on synthetic buyers and willingness-to-pay ceilings, and estimates net recovery as proposed price minus modelled costs. A pilot would replace these with customer research, measured conversion and actual reverse-logistics costs.
